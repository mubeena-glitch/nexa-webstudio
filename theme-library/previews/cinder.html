Our Story

We do one thing. Smoke meat properly, and refuse to rush it.

Cinder started as a single oil-drum smoker behind a railway arch in Hackney. Fifteen years on we still run the pit by hand, still light it before dawn, and still serve until the day's cook runs out.

Nothing is held overnight. Nothing is microwaved. When the brisket is gone, it is gone, and we start again tomorrow with a fresh fire.

The Method

From cold cut to cinder bark.

01

Light the pit

The fire is lit at four in the morning and brought slowly to a steady 110 degrees before a single cut goes on.

02

Smoke and wait

Brisket sits for sixteen hours. We open the lid as little as possible. Every look costs you heat and time.

03

Rest and slice

Off the pit, the meat rests for an hour so the juices settle, then we slice it to order in front of you.
