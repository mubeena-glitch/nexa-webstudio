A note from the editors

On the patience of making books

We began in a single rented room above a bookshop, persuaded that the printed page deserved more care than the market was willing to give it. Three decades on, the conviction has not softened. Every title we publish is read aloud at least once before it goes to press, because a sentence that stumbles in the ear will stumble on the page.

Our editors work slowly and argue often. We commission translations that take years, not seasons, and we pay for them properly. Paper is chosen by hand, the cloth on our hardbacks sourced from the same mill in the north for twenty years, and the typesetting is done by people who can tell you why a comma matters.

None of this is nostalgia. It is simply the belief that a reader who has paid attention deserves a book that has paid attention back. We would rather publish twelve volumes a year that endure than a hundred that are forgotten by spring.

If that sounds like a slow way to run a publishing house, it is. We have made our peace with it, and so, we hope, will you, somewhere around the third chapter, when the noise of the world falls away and only the page remains.

How a book is made here

From manuscript to keepsake

Every stage is deliberate. We treat the object and the text as one inseparable thing.

  • 01

    Read aloud, then again

    Each manuscript is voiced in full before acquisition, so cadence and clarity decide what we publish.

  • 02

    Set by hand, kerned by eye

    Our compositors choose the face, the leading, and the margins to suit the book, never a house template.

  • 03

    Sewn, not glued

    Hardbacks are section-sewn and cloth-bound at the same northern mill we have used for twenty years.
